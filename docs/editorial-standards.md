\# Casa de SAM Editorial Standards



\## Purpose



The Casa de SAM blog exists to help families understand lifelong disability care, housing, planning, safety, dignity, and community.



Our articles should be valuable even to readers who never support, contact, or participate in Casa de SAM.



The blog is not primarily a marketing tool. It is a public resource for families asking difficult questions about the future.



\## Core Editorial Principle



Every article should leave the reader:



\- better informed;

\- less overwhelmed;

\- more prepared to ask useful questions;

\- and able to take at least one practical next step.



We do not manipulate fear. We help families respond to it thoughtfully.



\## The Casa de SAM Test



Before publishing, ask:



1\. Would we send this article to a frightened parent if Casa de SAM did not exist?

2\. Does the article answer the main search question within the first few paragraphs?

3\. Does the reader learn something useful that goes beyond a basic definition?

4\. Does the article acknowledge trade-offs, limitations, and uncertainty?

5\. Does it include reliable sources where outside facts, laws, programmes, or policies are discussed?

6\. Does it give the reader practical steps they can take this week?

7\. Does it include useful questions, checklists, resources, or planning tools?

8\. Does Casa de SAM appear as context rather than the answer to every problem?

9\. Does the article respect people with disabilities as adults with preferences, rights, relationships, and meaningful lives?

10\. Does the reader finish the article feeling more capable of moving forward?



If the answer to several of these questions is no, the article is not ready.



\## We Always



\- Answer the search query clearly and early.

\- Write for families first and search engines second.

\- Explain terms in plain language.

\- Distinguish between general guidance and location-specific rules.

\- Use primary and authoritative sources whenever possible.

\- Provide practical examples.

\- Include questions families can ask professionals or service providers.

\- Include realistic actions the reader can take this week.

\- Acknowledge that support needs vary widely.

\- Recognise that independence is not all-or-nothing.

\- Treat safety, dignity, freedom, belonging, and quality of life as connected goals.

\- Respect nonspeaking and intellectually disabled adults as people who communicate and have preferences.

\- Explain both benefits and limitations of housing and support models.

\- State clearly when legal, financial, medical, educational, or benefits advice requires a qualified local professional.

\- Link to related Casa de SAM articles when they genuinely help the reader.

\- Review older articles when laws, programmes, links, or policies may have changed.



\## We Never



\- Use fear to pressure a reader into supporting Casa de SAM.

\- Present Casa de SAM as the only good solution.

\- Shame parents for beginning late, feeling overwhelmed, or making imperfect decisions.

\- Suggest that needing lifelong support makes an adult less valuable.

\- Treat employment, speech, or independent living as measures of human worth.

\- Promise that a specific programme, benefit, housing model, or legal arrangement will work for every family.

\- Hide important limitations in order to make an option sound better.

\- Publish unsupported medical, legal, financial, or benefits claims.

\- Use vague filler merely to make an article longer.

\- repeat the same point in several sections without adding useful context;

\- publish a list of resources without explaining what each resource is for;

\- turn every section into a description of Casa de SAM;

\- use clickbait, exaggerated statistics, or manipulative urgency.



\## Standard Article Priorities



Most articles should include the following elements when relevant.



\### 1. The Reader's Question



Identify the actual concern behind the search.



A person searching for housing options may also be asking:



\- Will my child be safe?

\- What happens when I die?

\- Can we afford this?

\- How much support will they need?

\- What options exist besides living with family?

\- How do I begin planning?



The article should answer the stated question while recognising the concern underneath it.



\### 2. A Direct Answer



Give the reader a useful answer near the beginning.



Do not require the reader to move through a personal story, organisational history, or long introduction before learning the basics.



\### 3. Thorough Explanation



Go beyond a dictionary definition.



Explain:



\- what the option or concept means;

\- how it works;

\- who it may suit;

\- who may need something different;

\- what support it assumes;

\- what can go wrong;

\- what families should verify;

\- and how the decision may change over time.



\### 4. Trade-Offs and Limitations



Avoid presenting one model as universally ideal.



Discuss issues such as:



\- staffing;

\- cost;

\- waiting lists;

\- privacy;

\- transportation;

\- medical needs;

\- overnight support;

\- community access;

\- funding;

\- legal authority;

\- caregiver continuity;

\- and emergency backup.



\### 5. Practical Questions and Checklists



Whenever possible, give the reader material they can use.



Examples include:



\- questions to ask a housing provider;

\- documents to gather;

\- signs that more support may be needed;

\- issues to discuss with an attorney;

\- questions for a Medicaid or benefits office;

\- items to include in a care summary;

\- and factors to observe during a visit.



\### 6. What You Can Do This Week



Every major planning article should include a section with manageable actions.



These should be concrete and realistic.



Examples:



\- create a planning folder;

\- write down the person's current support needs;

\- call one local agency;

\- join one waiting list;

\- schedule one consultation;

\- identify one backup caregiver;

\- visit one programme;

\- practise one safety skill;

\- review one legal document;

\- or choose the next planning date.



The goal is not to solve the person's entire future in one week.



The goal is to help the reader take the next useful step.



\### 7. Resources



Resource sections should prioritise:



1\. government agencies;

2\. official programme information;

3\. established disability organisations;

4\. legal-aid and professional directories;

5\. practical planning tools;

6\. local search guidance.



Each link should explain what the reader will find there and why it may help.



When resources apply only to one country, state that clearly.



\### 8. Casa de SAM Context



Casa de SAM may appear when it adds useful perspective.



It should usually appear after the article has already answered the reader's question.



The standard description should remain factual:



\- Casa de SAM is in the planning and organisational stage.

\- It is intended to become a lifelong residential community in Paraguay.

\- It is designed for adults with developmental disabilities who need ongoing support.

\- The long-term opening goal is 2035.

\- Casa de SAM is not yet a registered nonprofit.

\- Casa de SAM is not currently accepting donations.



The call-to-action components are managed separately and should not be hard-coded into the article copy.



\## Sources and Accuracy



Use primary sources whenever practical.



Preferred sources include:



\- government agencies;

\- official statutes or programme pages;

\- recognised disability-rights organisations;

\- established medical or educational institutions;

\- and original research.



Secondary sources may be used when they add helpful interpretation, but they should not replace available primary information.



When information can change by date or location, state that clearly.



Examples include:



\- Medicaid;

\- Social Security;

\- housing programmes;

\- guardianship law;

\- supported decision-making law;

\- education transition rules;

\- disability benefits;

\- tax rules;

\- and nonprofit status.



Do not imply that general internet information replaces professional advice.



\## Tone



The Casa de SAM voice is:



\- practical;

\- compassionate;

\- direct;

\- calm;

\- honest;

\- parent-informed;

\- and respectful.



We may use personal experience, but personal experience should support the article rather than replace evidence.



We should sound like someone who has lived with these questions and has taken the time to research them carefully.



\## Disability Language



Use language that respects both identity-first and person-first preferences.



Depending on the sentence and topic, acceptable phrasing may include:



\- autistic adult;

\- adult with autism;

\- person with an intellectual disability;

\- disabled adult;

\- adult with developmental disabilities;

\- nonspeaking adult;

\- person who needs lifelong support.



Avoid language that reduces a person to a burden, tragedy, diagnosis, behaviour, or level of productivity.



Do not assume that speech is the only meaningful form of communication.



Do not assume that living independently is the highest possible outcome.



\## Search Engine Optimisation



SEO supports the article. It does not control the article.



Use:



\- a clear title;

\- one primary search question;

\- a descriptive URL;

\- a useful meta description;

\- logical headings;

\- relevant internal links;

\- accurate image alt text;

\- structured data;

\- and trustworthy external sources.



Do not:



\- stuff keywords;

\- repeat phrases unnaturally;

\- add empty sections for word count;

\- write several paragraphs before answering the question;

\- or sacrifice clarity for search terminology.



\## Article Review Checklist



Before publishing, confirm that the article includes:



\- \[ ] A clear search-focused title

\- \[ ] A direct answer near the beginning

\- \[ ] A descriptive subtitle

\- \[ ] A useful introduction

\- \[ ] Logical H2 and H3 headings

\- \[ ] Substantial practical information

\- \[ ] Important trade-offs or limitations

\- \[ ] At least one checklist or question set

\- \[ ] A “What You Can Do This Week” section

\- \[ ] Trustworthy resources where appropriate

\- \[ ] Clear location or date limitations

\- \[ ] A professional disclaimer where necessary

\- \[ ] A factual Casa de SAM description

\- \[ ] Relevant internal links

\- \[ ] A helpful image caption

\- \[ ] Accurate alt text

\- \[ ] A meta description

\- \[ ] A canonical URL

\- \[ ] Open Graph information

\- \[ ] Article publication dates

\- \[ ] JSON-LD structured data

\- \[ ] Working CTA placeholders

\- \[ ] A final read for tone, accuracy, and usefulness



\## Final Standard



A Casa de SAM article should not merely rank for a question.



It should deserve to rank because it answers that question with depth, care, practical guidance, reliable resources, and respect for the families and disabled adults whose lives are affected by it.
